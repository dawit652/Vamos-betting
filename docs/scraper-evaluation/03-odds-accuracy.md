## 2. Method

**Data (untrusted, read-only, kept outside the repository):**

- Results: openfootball `football.json` at `master` on 2026-10-29: `2025-26/en.1`, `2024-25/en.1`, `2023-24/en.1` (backfill source), `es.1`, `de.1`, `it.1`, `fr.1` for 2025-26 and 2024-25, and `2026-27/en.1` (fixtures for the integrity run). The Premier League scores were checked against the football-data.co.uk results copy below: 380 of 380 matches compared in each of 2024-25 and 2025-26, 0 mismatches.
- **Bookmaker closing odds were found:** GitHub repository `AnishKhetani/premier-league-data` (commit `d69530c645c10c7844ee416a6af56a4b392e37be`, a football-data.co.uk mirror), file `data/processed/results_with_odds.csv`, columns `market_avg_*_close`, `pinnacle_*_close`, `bet365_*_close` for 1X2 and over/under 2.5. Join by season plus home and away club: 380 of 380 matches in both seasons (join rate 100%, 0 date mismatches). Coverage: average and bet365 closing odds for 760 matches; Pinnacle closing for 590 (all of 2024-25, 210 of 2025-26). The file's data licence is the original site's; only aggregates are published here, not the odds.
- The Premier League 2025-26 file lists 27 goalless draws as a bare `[0, 0]` list; the scraper adapter rejects it (`bad_score`, `packages/scraper/src/adapters/openfootball.ts`). All 27 are real 0-0 results (checked against football-data.co.uk, 27 of 27). In my "truth" world they count as results; in a "visible" world (what the scraper would learn from) they are skipped. For other leagues a bare `[0, 0]` dated on or before the newest proper result of the file is treated as an inferred 0-0 (unverified; 15, 12, 36 and 24 matches in La Liga, Bundesliga, Serie A and Ligue 1; in the Premier League the same rule was right 27 of 27 times).

**Walk-forward loop.** Matches in date and time order. Before each match, `priceFixture('football', home, away)` with the current ratings; then `updateFootballRatings` with default options. The scored 1X2 probabilities are the offered odds with the margin removed **proportionally** (p_i = (1/o_i) / sum(1/o_j)). The model defines odds = 1 / (p (1 + margin)) with margin 6%, so this equals inverting the margin exactly and renormalising; hundredth rounding is absorbed by the renormalisation. Over 2.5 and BTTS are taken the same way from the two-way markets. Expected goals come from `matchStrengths`.

Start conditions: (a) neutral ratings for every club; (b) the catalogue's seeded ratings (`packages/db/src/seed/catalog.ts`) for clubs it has, neutral for the others (all 20 clubs in 2025-26; 17 of 20 in 2024-25; clubs of other leagues only partly); (c) neutral, then a replay of the previous season (`replayRatings`, the same as `ratings backfill`, which starts every team neutral). The seed was written before this evaluation but may have been informed by recent seasons; (b) can therefore be optimistic.

**Metrics.** Brier (three-class sum), log-loss, ranked probability score (home, draw, away order). Baselines: uniform, and walk-forward league base rates (home, draw, away frequencies learned so far, add-one smoothing). Paired differences by bootstrap. Reliability: equal-width probability deciles per outcome; ECE is the count-weighted mean absolute gap in percentage points, with a bootstrap interval (it is biased upward at small n, which matters for a single season). Learning curve: by month of season.

**Market comparison.** Margin removal of closing odds: proportional and power method (p_i = (1/o_i)^k, k solved so the sum is 1). Relative log-loss difference (model - market) / market with bootstrap. Edge test: slope of (outcome - market probability) on (model - market probability), plus binned means and a log-loss blend. Customer test: bet one unit on every selection whose offered milli-odds are at least the market's fair odds (proportional, average of bookmakers), return at our odds.

**Price integrity.** A grid of 9^4 = 6561 rating combinations (attack and defence of both teams from {0.35, 0.5, 0.7, 0.85, 1, 1.2, 1.6, 2.2, 3}); named scenarios; malformed ratings; the 380 real 2026-27 Premier League fixtures with seeded ratings (the 108 fixtures with Coventry, Hull or Ipswich are priced with neutral ratings for the missing clubs because the catalogue at the evaluated commit lacks them). Checks are the production `checkMarkets` (O2) and independent checks done in interval arithmetic: a published price can only come from probabilities inside a known interval (odds = floor of 1/(p (1+m)) to a hundredth, a 1.01 floor, a 1.001 fallback above p = 0.990, a 1000.00 cap), so a "violation" means no probability vector could have produced the book. Declared margins: 600 bps, 1500 bps for correct score. The overround of a market is sum(1/o) divided by the number of selections that win per outcome (2 for double chance), compared with 1.06 (1.15 correct score).

**Re-pricing.** Season replay in time order. An event exists from 28 days (`LOOK_AHEAD_MS`) to 10 minutes (`MIN_LEAD_MS`) before kickoff; results arrive 2 hours after kickoff. Each result re-prices the open events of both teams (ratings fingerprint changed) and the production `checkReprice` decides (limit 1.25). Two trader behaviours: approves every hold, or ignores them. Only calibrated teams (O1, 8 matches) are re-priced. Run on condition (c).

This is a selection effect of any softer-than-market price, and it measures the cost of showing prices that are on average 2% worse than the line without a trader overlay. It is an upper bound for a customer who has perfect access to closing lines; closing lines are later than the prices we would publish days ahead.

### 3.5 Price integrity

- **Zero violations** of: selection sets (exactly the contract's selections), odds bounds (integers 1.001 to 1000.000), whole-hundredth rounding (above 1.01), probability sums (every book is consistent with probabilities summing to 1, double chance to 2), no sure-win book (the smallest overround per winning selection was 1.00007 for an over/under line, 1.00075 for 1X2), double chance = the right combination of 1X2, draw no bet = home / (home + away), correct-score grid plus "other" brackets every result and agrees with 1X2, BTTS no and every under line, over/under odds monotonic in the line, every cross-line and cross-market cover (for example over 1.5 plus under 2.5) costs at least 1, and exact mirror symmetry (swapping expected goals swaps 1/2, 1X/X2, correct-score h-a/a-h, half-time and draw-no-bet selections; 2825 comparisons, none broken). There is no handicap market, so handicap symmetry does not apply.
- **Rounding cannot push a book below 1.00**: prices are rounded down to the hundredth, which only raises the overround (by up to +0.97 pp on 1X2).
- **Margin is not the declared margin** (the gap): the declared 6% is delivered only in the middle of the range.
  - Above +0.5 pp (rounding down to a hundredth on short prices): 1X2 10.6% of grid pairs, double chance 20%, draw no bet 18%; real PL fixtures 1X2 1.3% (maximum +0.59 pp), double chance 21.6%.
  - Below -0.5 pp (the 1.01 floor and the 1.001 fallback): 1X2 12.2% of grid pairs down to -5.9 pp; over/under lines 33% of grid lines (50% are outside in either direction) and 19.6% of the 1900 real PL lines, down to -4.4 pp (over 0.5 and over 1.5 for lopsided fixtures such as Arsenal v Coventry: 3.1% margin where 6% is declared). Heavy favourites above about p = 0.93 get a flat 1.01 and above p = 0.990 a flat 1.001, whatever the margin.
  - Correct score: 1500 bps declared; 34% of grid books above +0.5 pp (up to +1.9 pp), none below in real fixtures.
- **Gate O2** (`checkMarkets`, overround must be in [1.01, 1.30]) rejects the model's own book in 3778 of 6561 grid pairs (all with a heavy favourite or very low expected goals: an over/under or BTTS line ends below 1.01), and in 0 of the 380 real 2026-27 fixtures. It fails closed, so those events would be held for a trader, not published.
- **Missing markets:** for extreme pairs the model drops a market whose outcome is practically decided (over 0.5 in 959 grid books, double chance and draw no bet in 14, others rarely); none dropped for real PL fixtures. A dropped market changes the market set, which the re-price gate treats as `market_set_changed` (held).
- **Extreme and malformed ratings:** `priceFixture` never threw in 6561 pairs. Extreme values give expected goals clamped to [0.05, 6]: strongest home vs weakest away is 1.001 / 189 / 1000 (overround 1.0053) and fails O2. Missing, null, empty, zero, negative, NaN, Infinity, string or array ratings silently price as neutral 1/1 (expected goals 1.553 and 1.350, 1X2 2.23 / 3.83 / 2.84); only O1 stops such a book from being offered. A rating of 100 is not clamped by the pricing path (expected goals clamp at 6) and fails O2.
- **Home advantage at equal ratings:** fair 1X2 42.2% / 24.6% / 33.2% (home 2.23, draw 3.83, away 2.84 with margin); the observed league frequencies in the pool are 43.5% / 25.3% / 31.3%.

Slower rates (0.02 to 0.035) have the same or marginally better log-loss (differences of 0.001, far inside the interval of +-0.018) and cut the hold share from 21% to 2% to 7%. The offset (kOffset 3, 6, 12, 24) changes log-loss by at most 0.002 when backfilled and 0.012 from neutral (3 is worst).

### 3.7 O1: an uncalibrated team cannot be opened (code audit)

- The scraper opens betting in exactly one place: at event creation, `decision.openBetting` from `packages/scraper/src/gates/fixture.ts:353` (`autoOpen === true && calibrated && pricesSane`), applied in `apps/scraper/src/pipeline/promote-fixtures.ts:419` (`setEventBettingOpen(tx, eventId, decision.openBetting)`). `calibrated` comes from `isCalibrated` for both teams (`promote-fixtures.ts` `pricingFor`; the function treats missing or malformed ratings as uncalibrated and never accepts fewer than `MIN_RATING_MATCHES` = 8 matches, or `manual === 1`). Every other creation path returns `bettingOpen: false`.
- Re-pricing never opens betting (`apps/scraper/src/pipeline/reprice.ts:91-93`), skips teams that are not calibrated (`reprice.ts:230`) and only hands an event to a trader as an `odds_approval` item once both teams are calibrated (`reprice.ts:431` onward); an uncalibrated event has the item kind `ratings_uncalibrated`.
- The only decision that opens betting from a review item, `open_betting` (`apps/api/src/modules/admin/feed.ts:523`), is allowed only on `odds_approval` items (`FEED_RESOLVE_DECISION_KINDS`, `packages/contracts/src/feed.ts:587`), so it is refused (422 `decision_not_allowed`) for a `ratings_uncalibrated` item.
- Residual: calibration is checked when the item is created, not when the trader presses open, and the generic back-office endpoint (`apps/api/src/modules/admin/trading.ts:145` calling `staffSetEventBettingOpen`, `packages/db/src/backoffice/trading.ts:158`) opens betting on any scheduled event with no O1 or O2 check. A trader with `trading.status` can therefore open an uncalibrated, neutral-priced event. This is a human action, but it is outside the "never" in the rule. _Closed on 2026-10-30 (F-10, commit 9d64b56): both open controls re-check O1 and O2 when pressed._

## 4. Gaps, ranked

1. **Prices are exposed to a customer who knows the market line (high).** What: the model prices are on average 2.2% (log-loss) worse than the closing line and add no information to it. Evidence: customer return +12.5% [+1.8%, +23.3%] on 891 bets; blend never improves; slope 0.02 [-0.32, +0.38]. Impact: sharp or line-shopping customers win from us, mainly on away and total-goals selections; recreational customers lose the margin as expected (backing every outcome returned -5.8%, the declared 6%). Prices are not too generous on average, only where our price is longer than the line. Fix: keep scraped odds out of publication, but (i) add a trader or limit layer for stakes on prices that move late, (ii) calibrate the home advantage and the draw (gaps 3 and 4), and (iii) consider lowering exposure with a higher margin on over/under and double chance until the model is closer to the line. Complexity: M for (ii) and (iii), L for a real overlay. Recommended: opus high for the design, sonnet high for the parameter work.
2. **Goalless draws are not learned and cannot settle (high; shared with the data evaluation).** What: 27 of 380 PL 2025-26 results (7.1%) are bare `[0, 0]` lists the adapter rejects. Evidence: `packages/scraper/src/adapters/openfootball.ts` (`bad_score`), 27 of 27 verified real 0-0. Impact: those events never get a result, so bets on them do not settle by the scraper; ratings never see 0-0 (log-loss +0.003, expected goals drift up). Fix: accept `[h, a]` as a score only when a second independent source agrees (or when the match is older than a safe lag and the file is otherwise complete), and flag the format in the source health check. Complexity: S to M. Recommended: sonnet medium.
3. **Home advantage is a fixed 1.15 and the model prices away wins too short (medium).** Evidence: implied home/away goal ratio 1.146 vs observed 1.213 (pool) and 1.247 (PL 2025-26); away bias +2.92 pp [+1.05, +4.78]; return of backing away -14.8% [-21.3, -7.9]. Impact: away backers lose more than the margin, home backers less; customers win by backing home and draw selections, not by a significant amount in this sample. Fix: make `FOOTBALL_HOME_ADVANTAGE` (packages/feed/src/pricing/football.ts, 1.15) a per-league parameter learned from results (about 1.2 to 1.25 here), or learn a home-advantage term in `updateFootballRatings`; re-check calibration. Complexity: M. Recommended: sonnet high.
4. **Draw probability is too low and cannot be adjusted (medium).** Evidence: bias -1.65 pp [-3.52, +0.17] pooled, -3.36 pp in PL 2025-26; independent Poisson draws sit between 20% and 30% for 83% of matches. Impact: draws are slightly too generous (return +1.1% [-6.4, +8.7], not significant). Fix: add a draw inflation (Dixon-Coles style low-score correction, parameter rho) or a correlation term in `scoreMatrix` (packages/feed/src/pricing/football.ts). Complexity: M. Recommended: sonnet high.
5. **Declared margin is not what is delivered on short prices and lopsided over/under lines (medium).** Evidence: 1X2 overround outside 6% +- 0.5 pp in 22.8% of rating pairs (12.2% below to -5.9 pp), over/under lines 36% of real PL lines outside (19.6% below, to -4.4 pp), double chance and draw no bet 12.6% to 21.6% above. Impact: we give away part of the margin on favourites and on over 0.5 / 1.5 (a customer backing both sides of a lopsided over/under pair keeps up to 3 pp more), and charge up to 1 pp more than declared on others, so margin reporting is wrong by sign and size. Fix: price the margin on the whole book (distribute it across selections so the overround equals the declared value after rounding, for example a power or odds-ratio method) and round to the nearest hundredth toward the declared total; keep a minimum margin check in `checkMarkets` (O2 already requires 1.01, far below 1.06). Complexity: M. Recommended: sonnet high.
6. **Re-price holds are 21.6%, target 10% (medium).** Evidence: 493 of 2286 checks held, all from non-1X2 markets (1X2 never above 1.24); 12 events held per round (3.7 for the next match). Impact: a trader sees about 12 approvals per matchday and prices go stale (46% held if nobody acts); nothing is lost to customers but delay and workload. Fix: apply the 1.25 rule to 1X2 and over/under 2.5 only (or scale it with the price), or slow the learning rate (kMin 0.035 gives 7% holds with equal log-loss; `DEFAULT_RATING_K_MIN` in packages/feed/src/pricing/ratings.ts). Complexity: S (rate) or M (rule). Recommended: sonnet medium; the rate change needs an out-of-sample check first.
7. **A trader can open betting on an uncalibrated event through the generic endpoint (low).** Evidence: section 3.7. Fix: make `staffSetEventBettingOpen` (or the route) refuse an event of the scraped feed provider whose teams fail `isCalibrated` unless a note is given, and re-run `checkMarkets` at the time of opening. Complexity: S. Recommended: sonnet medium.
8. **O2 rejects the model's own output for lopsided matches, and malformed ratings price silently as neutral (low).** Evidence: 3778 of 6561 grid books rejected (0 of 380 real fixtures); null, zero, negative, NaN, string ratings price as 1/1 (section 3.5). Impact: a lopsided fixture (for example a top side against a promoted one with extreme ratings) would wait for a trader; neutral pricing is blocked only by O1. Fix: drop the markets that cannot hold the margin (over 0.5, over 1.5, BTTS for extreme expected goals) instead of failing the whole book, and let `priceFixture` throw on invalid ratings. Complexity: S to M. Recommended: sonnet medium.
9. **O/U and BTTS prices have no skill beyond league base rates (low).** Evidence: Brier 0.2499 vs 0.2492 (over 2.5), 0.2493 vs 0.2488 (BTTS), 2.8% worse than the line. Fix: a per-team pace or total-goals parameter, or shrink these prices toward the league base rate. Complexity: M. Recommended: sonnet high.

## 5. Limits

- Samples are small: one Premier League season is 380 matches, so the 95% intervals on log-loss differences are +-0.03, the per-decile tables have many bins under 30, and the single-season ECE is biased upward. The pool (2131 matches) mixes leagues and seasons and is dependent through shared clubs. Bootstrap resamples matches, not clubs.
- The market comparison covers only the Premier League (760 matches) and closing lines, which are better informed than any price we publish days ahead; "within 2%" is therefore a demanding target. Bookmaker odds were used as reference only and never as inputs.
- The seeded ratings may have been informed by 2024-25 and later results; condition (b) can be optimistic. Clubs missing from the catalogue (3 of 20 in 2024-25; many in other leagues) were priced neutral in (b).
- Condition (c) backfills from the previous season's file; clubs new to a league start neutral.
- Inferred 0-0 results in other leagues (87 matches in 2025-26) are not verified against a second source.
- The re-price simulation assumes results arrive 2 hours after kickoff, one re-price pass per result, events from 28 days before kickoff, and the two extreme trader behaviours; real timing differs (the promotion pass interval, delays in the data source). Holds are not attributed to individual markets. The learning-rate sweep uses the same data it is judged on.
- The overround analysis uses the declared margins (600 and 1500 bps) from the code defaults, not a deployment override.
- No live (in-play) prices, other sports, or database behaviour were evaluated. The O1 audit is a read of the code, not a test.
