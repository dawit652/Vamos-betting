## E2 design (23 Nov, second account; to confirm on one real answer first)

The pricing step reads only bookmaker prices of the 1X2 and the over/under (`market_odds`,
`packages/scraper/src/types.ts`). It derives our other markets from them, so E2 needs no new market
types.

- **Adapter.** `apifootball` gets a mode `odds`, one source per league (like `theoddsapi-*`), with
  its league id and season.
  - Each run reads the league's fixtures from today to 14 days ahead (team names: the odds answer
    carries only fixture ids), then the odds pages `/odds?league=&season=&page=1..3`.
  - Match Winner (Home, Draw, Away) becomes the 1X2. Goals Over/Under on half lines ("Over 2.5")
    becomes the over/under; other lines are dropped, as The Odds API's are.
  - Bookmakers are named like The Odds API's keys where they are the same book (Pinnacle →
    `pinnacle`), so a book listed by both upstreams counts once.
  - A fixture's odds older than 4 hours (`update`) or in the future are dropped: API-Football
    refreshes pre-match odds about every 3 hours.
- **Freshness.** Pricing judges freshness by when we read a price (`observed_at`, 3 hours; 30
  minutes within 3 hours of kickoff), so the odds cadence keeps a price current. The 4-hour cap
  keeps its content honest. Close to kickoff, Narobet's board (`narobet_pricing`, on) gives the
  fresher price for the matches it lists.
- **Shadow first.** A back office switch `apifootball_pricing`, off by default. While it is off,
  the prices are stored but pricing ignores them. A report section compares them with The Odds
  API's on the same matches. Switch it on once they agree, before the credits end.
- **Budget.** Standard odds intervals (30 minutes within 3 hours of kickoff, 2 hours within a day,
  then 170 minutes): about 1,000-1,500 requests a day on a busy day with E1, inside 7,500.
- **Tests.** Adapter unit tests on a scrubbed real answer, a pricing test that the switch hides
  them, and a run test.
